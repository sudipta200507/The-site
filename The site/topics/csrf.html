Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF)

CSRF forces a logged-in user's browser to execute unwanted actions on a web application.

6 min read

What is CSRF?

CSRF tricks a victim's browser into making an unintended request. It exploits the fact that browsers automatically include cookies with requests.

Analogy: You sign a paper at a bank without reading it - the bank trusts your signature. CSRF is like that forged request.

CSRF Attack Flow

Why This Matters for Pentesting

CSRF allows attackers to perform actions like transfer money, change password, or promote users to admin without user interaction.

Protection Methods

  • Anti-CSRF Tokens: Generate unique tokens and validate on state-changing requests
  • SameSite Cookie Attribute: Set cookies with SameSite=Strict or Lax
  • Origin Header Validation: Check Origin and Referer headers
